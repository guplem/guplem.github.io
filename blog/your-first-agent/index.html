Tutorial · from zero, no coding

Your first AI agent lives in a folder

This guide is for you if you use an AI in the browser, copy and paste a lot, and every chat starts from zero. Step by step, you will build your own agent: a folder on your computer with its instructions, its memory and its skills.

Guillem Poy 25 October 2026 15 minute read; an hour if you build it as you go

The short version, if you only have a minute:

  • A chat forgets; a folder remembers. If the agent saves what it learns in files in a folder, it still knows it tomorrow.
  • You need three things: a desktop app (Claude, ChatGPT, Antigravity or Goose), a folder, and a file of instructions called AGENTS.md.
  • What you learn works in every app. AGENTS.md and skills are standards. If you change apps, your agent is still yours.
  • At the end you will have an agent that works, built from one of five examples: student, teacher, health, journal or a home renovation.

From chat to agent

What you will build, and why it is worth it. You do not need to install anything yet.

When you use ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini in the browser, three things happen:

  • Every chat starts almost from zero. Some apps remember a little, but you do not decide what. You explain again who you are and what you want.
  • You are the messenger. You copy text from your files, paste it in the chat, copy the answer and paste it somewhere else.
  • It does not work with the files on your computer. It cannot read your notes, change your workout table, or create a document in your folder. Some apps connect to services like Google Drive, but what is on your computer stays out of reach.

An agent fixes all three. It is an AI model (the "brain" that understands and writes text) that can also act, not only talk: read files, write them, and follow on its own the instructions that you leave it in writing. To act, it needs an app on your computer that gives it those hands, and a folder to work in.

The endless chat trap

Because each new chat starts almost from zero, many people do the logical thing: they open one chat for one subject (their classes, their diet, their renovation…) and use it for weeks. That way they do not have to explain everything again. But this creates another problem, and almost nobody sees it: too much context works against you.

Everything the model reads to answer is called the context: its rules, the whole conversation and your new message. The context has a maximum size. When an endless chat fills it, the app must make room: depending on the app, it compacts the old messages into a summary, or stops reading them. It may not tell you, and details get lost on the way.

And even when everything fits, a huge context works worse. Think of it like this: if you read one page and I ask you something about it, you will almost surely get it right. If you read the whole Bible and I ask the same question, it is much easier to miss something. A model is the same: the longer the context, the harder it is to keep every detail in mind.

An agent with a folder solves this, because what matters lives in files, not in the chat. So you can open a new chat for each task, with a clean context, and the agent reads only the files that the task needs.

And on top of that, an agent can…

Beyond remembering, working in a folder opens up things that a chat does not do:

  • Create real files on your computer. A table for Excel, a page to print or a document to send. They are saved in your folder: they are yours, you open them with your usual programs, and you share them as you like.
  • Work with many files. In a chat you attach a few. In a folder you can have hundreds or thousands: 300 scanned documents, for example, whether notes, exams, receipts or quotes. The agent opens them one by one and leaves you a table or a summary with what you ask for. It takes time and uses up your plan, but there is no limit on how many files you can give it.
  • Use programs on your computer, if you allow it. It can even write its own: small programs, called scripts, that sort photos, convert documents or do sums. They solve the problem that models write the answer that seems most likely: that is why they sometimes slip on a long sum, or do a task a little differently each time. A script does it the same way every time, without mistakes.
  • Learn skills. You can write it skills: instructions for one task, which the agent follows when it needs them or when you ask. You will see them in step 6.
  • Work in parallel. You can open several chats at once on the same folder, each with its own task. Just make sure they do not undo each other's work.

So the agent you will build has three parts:

  • The model, the brain. A company (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google…) or an open project makes it. It usually runs on the internet, not on your computer.
  • The app, where you work together. A program on your computer. It is where you talk to the agent and see what it does, and it gives the agent its tools: read, create and change the files in its folder.
  • The folder, the home and the memory. It holds the agent's instructions, what it must remember and what it gives you. It is yours: you are in full control, and you can copy it and share it as you like.
What is a model (LLM), and why does it remember nothing?

A large language model (LLM) is a program trained on a huge amount of text. Given some text, it works out the most likely next words, one word at a time. That is how it writes its answers.

The important part for this guide: the model only knows what it learned in training and what you give it in each message. It remembers nothing by itself. That is why the folder is so useful.

To read more: Large language model, on Wikipedia.

Choose your example

From here on, you will build a real agent, step by step. To make each step concrete, choose an example now: the guide will show you how to build that agent. Pick the one closest to your case, or the one you like most.

The steps are the same for everyone; only what the agent knows how to do changes. With what you learn, you can later build any other: one for your finances, your vegetable garden, an exam you are preparing, or your business.

Without JavaScript you will see all five examples in each step, each with its name. Follow only yours.

You choose and install the program that connects the model to your folder.

Here I just call it "the app". If you see the word elsewhere, its technical name is a harness: the part that wraps the model and gives it tools.

You need a computer (Windows, Mac or Linux), not a phone: the agent works with the files on your computer.

For Mac, Windows and Linux. Free for personal use, with a weekly limit; with Google AI Pro or Ultra you get more use. Install the Antigravity app from antigravity.google/download.

Goose, open and free

For Mac, Windows and Linux. It is open source (anyone can read and reuse its code), and it works with almost any model: your ChatGPT Plus or Pro subscription, a key from OpenRouter, Anthropic or Google, or a model that runs on your own computer. With a Claude subscription it works only if you also install Claude Code from the terminal. Download it at goose-docs.ai.

Before you start, change one setting: by default, Goose acts without asking for permission. In "Settings > Chat > Default Mode", choose "Manual" (its docs call it "Manual Approval") or "Smart", so that it asks you before it touches your files.

What is an API key? (if you do not have one, you do not need one)

A subscription (ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro) is a monthly fee with a usage limit. An API key is a long password that gives access to a model, and you pay for what you use, cent by cent.

OpenRouter is a service that gives you one key for hundreds of models from many companies, and some of them are free. It is the simplest way to try different models with Goose.

Be careful with free models: some use what you send them to improve. Do not send them personal or work data.

There are more open apps. OpenWork is very easy to install, but it is young and it sometimes stops showing older chats after an update. Pi has many settings, but you use it from the terminal (the text window where you type commands); I will write a separate post about it.

Step 2 of 8

Create the folder

You create the agent's home.

A folder is a container on your computer that holds files. A file is a document with a name, and its ending (.md, .csv, .pdf) tells you what kind of content it holds.

The agent only sees what is inside its folder. That is good: you decide what it knows and what it does not. But remember that it sends what it reads to the model, on the internet, so that it can answer. Do not put anything in the folder that you would not tell that company.

  1. Create a folder on the Desktop (or in Documents, if you prefer). On a Mac: right-click on the Desktop, "New Folder". On Windows: right-click, "New", "Folder".
  2. Give it a name with no spaces and no accents, for example: Student my-study-tutor Teacher my-class-helper Health my-coach Journal my-journal Renovation bathroom-renovation . Simple names avoid problems with some apps.
  3. Inside it, create two folders: data and exports. The agent will create the rest later.

When you finish the guide, the folder will have these parts. Each one has a colour, the same everywhere on this page. Tap a part to see what it is for and who reads it.

What is a text file, and what are Markdown, CSV and JSON?

A file holds information. A text file holds only letters, with no hidden formatting, and any program can read it. A model loves it: it reads it just like you do. A Word document, on the other hand, holds much more than the text, and it is harder for a program to read.

  • Markdown (.md): text with a few simple marks. # is a title, - is a list item, **word** is bold. It is the best format for instructions and notes.
  • CSV (.csv): a table written as text, with one row per line and the columns separated by commas. It opens in Google Sheets, or in Excel with "Data > From Text/CSV". It is the best format for logs that you want to add up or draw in a chart.
  • JSON (.json): data with a fixed structure, such as {"topic": 3, "status": "mastered"}. It is the best format when a program, such as a script, must always read the data in the same way.

A practical rule: start with Markdown. Move to CSV when you want to count or make charts.

Did you know? If you copy the folder to another computer, the agent travels with it: its instructions and its memory are inside. And if you prefer to start from a ready-made one, download your example and keep reading to understand each part.

Step 3 of 8

Open the folder in your app and say hello

You connect the app to the folder and check that the agent can see it.

From here on, the agent does almost everything: you tell it what you want, and it creates the files.

Claude

Open the Claude app and go to Code. Under the message box, choose "Local" in the first selector; then, in the folder selector, click "Open folder…" and choose yours. Next to the message box is the permission mode: start with "Manual", which asks you before each change. The button names can change a little between versions.

ChatGPT

Open the ChatGPT app and choose Codex in the mode selector. Click "Add new project" and choose your folder. Leave the permission mode on "Ask for approval": it works inside your folder and asks you before it goes outside it.

Antigravity

Open the Antigravity app. Click the new project icon (a folder with a +), then "Add Folder", choose your folder and click "Create". If it asks where to work, choose "Local mode": your own folder. For permissions, start with "Request Review", which asks you before each command and before it leaves your folder or uses the internet.

Goose

Open Goose. In the bar at the bottom there is a folder selector: choose yours. Before you type, check the permission mode: in "Settings > Chat > Default Mode", choose "Manual", which asks you before it changes a file.

Now write your first message:

Hello. What is in this folder?

You will see something like this. The agent uses its tools (read, write, search) and shows you each step:

Hello. What is in this folder?

looked at the folder
There are two empty folders: data and exports. There are no instructions yet. What do you want me to do?

The robot is the agent; the person on the right is you.

Each app has a permission mode: it decides what the agent does on its own and what it asks you first. At the start, choose the most careful mode of your app (the one named above). Some ask you before every change; others change files inside your folder without asking, but ask you before they leave it, use the internet or run programs. When it asks for permission, read what it wants to do and accept if it makes sense. For example, if it asks to create the file that you just asked for, accept. When you trust it, you can give it more freedom inside that folder.

If it does not see the folder, check that you chose the right folder and not the whole Desktop. On a Mac, the first time, the system asks if the app can access the Desktop: click "Allow". If you said no, go to "System Settings > Privacy & Security > Files and Folders", and turn on your app.

Step 4 of 8

Give it instructions: the AGENTS.md file

You tell the agent who it is and how it must work, one time and for good.

That goes in a file called AGENTS.md, at the root of the folder (directly inside it, not in a subfolder).

Remember the context, from the start: the model remembers nothing between messages. Each time you write, the app sends it everything it must keep in mind, all at once. This is what goes in:

(Skills are instructions for tasks that you repeat; you will see them in step 6.) That is why AGENTS.md is so powerful: it is in the context of every conversation. What you write there, the agent always keeps in mind. And it is an open standard: Claude, ChatGPT (Codex), Antigravity, Goose and many other apps read it. So you do not depend on any one app.

Your AGENTS.md

This is the AGENTS.md for your example. Read it: it says who the agent is, what it does, where it keeps each thing, and a few rules. Change the details that are yours (your school year, your groups, your goal) and keep the structure.

Student

AGENTS.md
# My study tutor

You are my personal tutor. I am in the last year of high school.
You speak to me in English, with short sentences and examples.

## What you do
- You explain the topics in a simple way, with examples.
- When I ask, you give me exam-style questions, one at a time, and you correct me.
- You keep track of what I already know well and what I find hard.
- You remind me of the assignments that are due.

## Where you keep things
- data/subjects.md: my subjects, their topics and the exam dates.
- data/progress.md: a table with each topic, its status (new, in progress, mastered), the date of the last review and what I find hard.
- data/assignments.md: each assignment with its due date and its status.
- data/notes/: my notes, one file per topic.
- exports/: the practice exams and the summaries.

## Rules
- Before you answer, read what you need from data/.
- At the end of each study session, update data/progress.md.
- If something is not in my notes, tell me. Do not make it up.
- To prepare a practice exam, use the practice-exam skill.

Teacher

AGENTS.md
# My class helper

You are my class helper. I teach maths in Year 4 (4th grade), to two groups: 4A and 4B.
You speak to me in English, in a clear and practical way.

## What you do
- You make worksheets with exercises that fit the level of each group.
- You keep track of how each group is doing: which topics we have covered and what is hardest.
- You help me plan the week.

## Where you keep things
- data/groups.md: each group, the current topic and what it finds hardest.
- data/grades.csv: the grades, with the columns student,group,activity,date,grade. One row per student and activity.
- data/calendar.md: the planned lessons and assessments.
- exports/: the worksheets and the plans, ready to print.

## Rules
- Never save the full names of students. Use their class list number (A01, A02…).
- The worksheets have the answers on a separate page.
- Before you prepare anything, read data/groups.md.
- To prepare a worksheet, use the make-worksheet skill.

Health

AGENTS.md
# My coach

You are my personal coach. My goal is in data/goal.md.
You speak to me in English, with encouragement but without exaggeration.

## What you do
- You log each workout and each meal that I tell you about.
- You suggest workouts based on my routine and on how I am doing.
- Each week you tell me how close I am to my goal, with numbers.

## Where you keep things
- data/goal.md: my goal, my starting point and the deadline.
- data/log.csv: one row per entry, with the columns date,type,detail,amount,unit. For example: 2026-10-20,run,easy,5,km. The type is run, gym or meal. A run takes two rows, one in km and one in min. Gym goes in min and meals go in kcal.
- data/routine.md: my weekly routine.
- exports/: the weekly summaries.

## Rules
- Write in data/log.csv as soon as I tell you something. Do not wait until the end.
- If I ask you something medical, or I tell you about a pain that does not go away, tell me to ask a professional.
- For the weekly summary, use the weekly-summary skill.

Journal

AGENTS.md
# My journal

You are my personal journal. I tell you how my day went and you put it in order.
You speak to me in English, calmly. You do not judge: you listen and you put things in order.

## What you do
- You save what I tell you each day, in my own words.
- You ask me one or two questions if I want to go deeper, never more.
- When I ask, you help me see what repeats: people, topics, moods.

## Where you keep things
- data/entries/: one file per day, with the date as its name (2026-10-25.md).
- data/themes.md: the topics and the people that come up often.
- exports/: the summaries that I ask for.

## Rules
- Each entry starts with two lines: the date (# 2026-10-25) and the mood in one word (Mood: calm).
- Do not change what I wrote: if you summarise it, save the summary separately.
- To close the day, use the close-the-day skill.

Renovation

AGENTS.md
# Bathroom renovation

You are the project manager for my bathroom renovation. I know nothing about building work: explain everything to me.
You speak to me in English, in a clear and orderly way.

## What you do
- You keep the list of renovation steps and their status.
- You keep the contacts of the professionals and what we discussed with each one.
- You keep the budget: what we planned and what we spent.
- You warn me about anything that blocks the next step.

## Where you keep things
- data/steps.md: each step in order, with its status (to do, in progress, done) and what it depends on.
- data/contacts.md: each professional, their phone number and a summary of each conversation.
- data/measurements.md: the bathroom measurements that I give you.
- data/budget.csv: one row per item, with the columns item,planned,spent.
- data/ideas/: photos and notes with ideas for the design.
- exports/: lists to take to the shop, summaries, simple plans.

## Rules
- Before you answer, read data/steps.md.
- Do not accept a measurement that I did not give you.
- To prepare a call with a professional, use the prepare-call skill.

To create it, the easiest way is to ask the agent. Click "Copy" on the text above. Then write this message and, under it, paste the text with Ctrl + V (Cmd + V on a Mac):

Create an AGENTS.md file in this folder with this content:

(paste the text here)

Claude

Claude reads the AGENTS.md too (since its September 2026 version), as long as there is no CLAUDE.md file. If your Claude does not read it, ask: "Create a CLAUDE.md file that contains only the line @AGENTS.md". That line tells it to load your AGENTS.md, and the other apps ignore the CLAUDE.md.

If you create it by hand on Windows, make sure that it does not end in .txt: Windows hides the end of file names, and the agent would not find the file. Further down, in "I prefer to create it by hand", you can see how to avoid this.

Now start a new conversation, because the app reads the AGENTS.md at the start. Ask:

Who are you and what can you do?

If it answers with what you put in the AGENTS.md, it works.

I prefer to create it by hand. How?

You need a plain text editor:

  • On a Mac, TextEdit: create a new document and choose "Format > Make Plain Text". Paste the text and save it as AGENTS.md inside your folder. If it asks, choose "Use .md".
  • On Windows, Notepad: paste the text, choose "Save As", set "Save as type" to "All files" and type AGENTS.md. If you do not, Windows saves it as AGENTS.md.txt and the agent does not find it.

The name matters: AGENTS in capitals and .md in lower case. To see the end of file names on Windows: in File Explorer, "View > Show > File name extensions".

What makes a good AGENTS.md?
  • Short. It is in the context of every conversation, so every line counts. If it grows a lot, move details to a skill.
  • Concrete. "Update data/progress.md at the end of each session" works; "be organised" does not.
  • With a map. Say where each thing is. The agent does not have to guess.
  • Alive. When the agent does something wrong, do not correct it only in the chat. Ask it: "Add this rule to the AGENTS.md so that it does not happen again".

Step 5 of 8

Give it memory: the data folder

You create the files where the agent keeps what it must remember.

This is what turns a chat that forgets into an agent that knows you.

Your AGENTS.md already says which files to use and when to update them. So you only have to ask:

Create the data files that the AGENTS.md lists. To fill them in, ask me the questions you need, one at a time.

The agent will ask you questions and create the files. In your example:

Student

  • data/subjects.md: your subjects, their topics and the exam dates.
  • data/progress.md: a table with each topic, its status, the date of the last review and what you find hard.
  • data/assignments.md: each assignment, its due date and its status.
  • data/notes/: save your notes here, as text or PDF. If they are photos, move them to the computer and save them here: the apps in this guide also read images, if the model you use supports them.

Teacher

  • data/groups.md: each group, the current topic and what it finds hardest.
  • data/grades.csv: the grades, with students by number, never by name.
  • data/calendar.md: the lessons and the assessments.

Grades are data about children. Before you save them, read your school's rules about AI tools. If you are not sure, save only how each group is doing, with no individual grades.

Health

  • data/goal.md: your goal, where you start from and the date.
  • data/log.csv: one row per entry (a run takes two rows: km and minutes).
  • data/routine.md: your weekly routine.

Journal

  • data/entries/: one file per day, for example 2026-10-25.md.
  • data/themes.md: the topics and the people that repeat.

Renovation

  • data/steps.md: the steps in order, with their status.
  • data/contacts.md: the professionals and what you discussed with each one.
  • data/measurements.md: the bathroom measurements, taken by you.
  • data/budget.csv: what you planned and what you spent, item by item.
  • data/ideas/: save photos and notes of bathrooms that you like here.

Open the files yourself too. A .md file opens in any text editor (TextEdit, Notepad); a .csv file opens in Excel or Google Sheets. They are yours: if something is wrong, correct it by hand or tell the agent. And if one day you want to start again from zero, delete them.

Why is ChatGPT's memory not enough? ChatGPT's memory is a short list of notes that the app keeps, and it only works in that app. You can read your files in full, correct them and take them to any app.

Step 6 of 8

Teach it skills

You teach the agent a task that you repeat, step by step, so that it always does it equally well.

That is a skill: saved instructions for one task.

You could put all the steps in the AGENTS.md, but they would take up the context of every conversation, even the ones that do not need them. A skill is smarter: the agent only sees its name and one sentence that describes it. When you ask for something that matches that sentence, it opens the full recipe and follows it.

A skill is a folder with a SKILL.md file inside. The folder has the same name as the skill. That file starts with a block between two --- lines with the name and the description, and then come the steps. The skills in the agent's folder go here:

Claude

.claude/skills/, and inside it one folder per skill. Claude does not look in .agents/skills/.

ChatGPT

.agents/skills/, and inside it one folder per skill.

Antigravity

.agents/skills/, and inside it one folder per skill.

Goose

.agents/skills/, and inside it one folder per skill. Goose also reads .claude/skills/.

On a Mac, the dot at the start hides the folder. Finder does not show folders whose name starts with a dot, and it does not let you create them. To see them, press Cmd + Shift + . in Finder. On Windows you see them normally. The easiest way, on both: let the agent create it.

This is the skill for your example:

Student

practice-exam/SKILL.md
---
name: practice-exam
description: Prepares a practice exam on the topics I ask for, with questions like the ones in class. Use it when I want to practise for an exam.
---

# Prepare a practice exam

1. Ask me which subject and which topics are in the exam, if I have not said so.
2. Read my notes on those topics in data/notes/ and their status in data/progress.md.
3. Write 10 questions: 4 on the topics I find hardest and 6 on the rest. Mix short questions and longer written ones.
4. Save the exam in exports/exam-SUBJECT-DATE.md, with the answers at the end.
5. Ask me if I want to do it now with you, one question at a time.
6. When we finish, update data/progress.md with what I got right and wrong.

Teacher

make-worksheet/SKILL.md
---
name: make-worksheet
description: Prepares a worksheet of exercises for a group and a topic, ready to print. Use it when I ask for a worksheet or exercises for class.
---

# Make a worksheet

1. Ask me the group and the topic, if I have not said so.
2. Read data/groups.md to know the level of the group and what it finds hardest.
3. Write 8 exercises, from easiest to hardest. The first two come with a worked example.

The rules for a skill
  • The name is in lower case, with no accents or spaces, and with hyphens: practice-exam. It is the same as the name of its folder.
  • The description says what the skill does and when to use it. It is the only thing the agent sees until it opens the skill, so write it with the words you will use when you ask for it.
  • Do not use a colon (:) inside the description. The block at the start uses colons to separate each field from its value, and one more colon can break it.
  • The steps are normal instructions, like the ones you would give a person.
  • It can hold more files in its folder: a template, an example, a script.

It is an open standard, Agent Skills. That is why the same skill works in every app in this guide.

Step 7 of 8 · optional

Fixed tools: the scripts folder

You give the agent a tool that always gives the same result. You can skip this step.

A model writes very well, but it calculates like a person in a hurry: almost always right, sometimes wrong. A script is a small program that does an exact calculation, the same way every time. You do not have to write it or run it: the agent does that.

Which format should you ask for?

  • Markdown (.md) to read it yourself or to give it to another agent.
  • An HTML page (.html) for something that looks nice: it opens in the browser and you print it from there.
  • CSV (.csv) to open in Excel or Google Sheets. If Excel puts everything in one column, use "Data > From Text/CSV" and choose the comma as the separator.
  • PDF or Word if your app can create them. Ask it: "Can you create a PDF?".

    Adapt it to your case

    Create an agent for something that is not in the five examples: your job, a hobby, a project.

    The steps are the same; only the AGENTS.md changes.

    The easiest way is to let the agent interview you. In a new folder, open it and write:

    I want an agent that helps me with (your task). Ask me questions, one at a time, about what I do and what I need. When you have enough, write an AGENTS.md with this template and create the folders that it names.

    And paste this template under it:

    AGENTS.md template
    # (Your agent's name)

You are (who it is: my helper for…, my tutor for…).
You speak to me in (language), (in what tone).

## What you do
- (The three or four tasks that you will ask for most.)

## Where you keep things
- data/(file).md: (what it holds).
- exports/: (what you give me).

## Rules
- (What it must always do.)
- (What it must never do.)

    Some ideas to get you started:

    • Human resources: the open hiring processes, their stages and the questions for each interview, with candidates by code, never by name.
    • Research: the log of field samples in a CSV, and a skill that prepares the report for each field trip.
    • A hobby: your vegetable garden, your recipes or your trips, with what worked and what did not.

    Use it every day

    The habits that make the agent better the more you use it.

    • One conversation per task. When you finish something, start a new one. The memory is in the files, not in the chat, so you lose nothing, and the context stays clean.
    • Correct the file, not the chat. If it does something wrong, ask it to add a rule to the AGENTS.md or to improve the skill.
    • Ask it what to save. At the end of a session: "Is there anything from today that you should save in data?".
    • Look at its files from time to time. They are your agent. The better they are, the better it works.
    • Grow little by little. When you repeat a task three times, turn it into a skill.

    Limits and warnings

    What this agent does not do, and what you should know before you trust it with things.

    • What is in the folder goes to the model. To answer, the app sends parts of your files to the company that runs the model. Do not keep passwords there, or data about other people who have not given you permission.
    • If it is for work, ask first. Check if your company has rules about AI and a company account (for example Claude Team or ChatGPT Business). Do not save data about colleagues with their names: use codes, as the teacher example does.
    • It makes mistakes. Check the important things: a health figure, a building measurement, a student's grade.
    • The apps have usage limits. With a free or cheap plan, you can reach the limit on a day of heavy use. Wait a few hours or change apps: the folder works in all of them. If you move to Claude, or away from Claude, ask the agent to copy the skills to the folder that the new app uses.
    • Make copies. If the folder is in Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox, you have a copy and you can open it on another computer. That also uploads your data to that service, and on a Mac the Desktop can be synced with iCloud without you knowing. If you know how to use Git, even better: it saves every change.
    • On two computers, one at a time. The folder travels, but the conversations and the app settings stay on each computer. Add the folder in the app on each one, work on only one at a time, and wait until it finishes syncing. If a "conflicted" file appears, ask the agent to merge the two.

    And from your phone? Your agent lives on your computer, so your phone does not see it. There are workarounds: if you leave the computer on with the app open, Claude and Antigravity (with their "Remote Control") and ChatGPT (with Codex in the phone app, on iPhone or Android) let you continue the conversation from your phone. Goose does not. And if the folder is in Google Drive, you can open it on another computer. But the natural place for this agent is the computer.

    Glossary

    The key words of the guide, in one sentence each. In the text, move the mouse over a word in bold, or tap it on your phone, to see its definition.

    Model (LLM)
    The brain: a program trained on a huge amount of text that understands and writes. For example Claude, GPT or Gemini. LLM stands for "large language model".
    App or harness
    The desktop program that connects the model to your computer: it gives the model tools and passes it your instructions. For example Claude, ChatGPT in Codex mode, Antigravity or Goose.
    Agent
    A model that, thanks to the app, can act on its own over several steps: read, decide, write and check.
    Folder
    A container on your computer that holds files. The agent's folder is its home: it only sees what is inside.
    File
    A document with a name. Its ending (.md, .csv, .json) tells you what kind of content it holds.
    AGENTS.md
    A text file with the agent's instructions, at the root of its folder. It is an open standard that almost every app reads.
    Context
    Everything the model reads before it answers: the app's rules, the AGENTS.md, the name and description of each skill, the conversation and your message. The model does not know anything that is not in the context. The longer it is, the harder it is to keep every detail in mind.
    Compact
    What the app does when a conversation no longer fits in the context: it summarises the old messages, or stops reading them, to make room. Details get lost on the way.
    Skill
    Instructions for a task that you repeat: a folder with a SKILL.md file (name, description and steps). The agent only reads the description until it needs the skill.
    Script
    A small program that does one exact task, the same way every time, such as adding up a column in a table.
    API key
    A long password that gives access to a model from any app. You pay for what you use, instead of a monthly fee. OpenRouter gives one key for hundreds of models.
    Subscription
    A monthly fee, such as ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro, with a usage limit. It almost always works only in that company's apps; Goose can sign in with ChatGPT Plus or Pro.
    Git
    A program that saves the history of every change in a folder, so that you can go back. The history also keeps the data, so treat it with the same care.
    Tool
    An action that the app lets the model do: read a file, write it, search or run a script.

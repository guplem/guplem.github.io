# Biology, topic 3: Genetics

- A gene is a piece of DNA that gives an instruction, for example eye colour.
- Each gene has two copies, the alleles: one from the mother and one from the father.
- A dominant allele (A) always shows. A recessive allele (a) only shows if there are two (aa).
- Genotype: the alleles you have (Aa). Phenotype: what you can see.
- Mendel's first law: if you cross two pure lines (AA and aa), all the children are Aa and look like the dominant one.
- Punnett square: a table to see all the possible children. Aa x Aa gives 25% AA, 50% Aa and 25% aa.
- Mendel's second law: in the cross Aa x Aa, the recessive trait comes back in 1 of every 4 children.
- Cross with two genes (AaBb x AaBb): the phenotype comes out in the ratio 9:3:3:1.
